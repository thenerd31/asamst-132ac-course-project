Section 08

Counter-Organizations and Resistance

The legal advocates, footballers, basketball players, lawyers, and political voices contesting the apparatus, and the dense network they have built.

Despite (and partly because of) escalating restrictions, French Muslim civil society and its allies have built a dense network of legal, advocacy, and grassroots organizations.

Legal Advocacy

Action Droits des Musulmans (ADM) filed the September 2023 challenge to the abaya ban at the Council of State and continues to litigate discrimination cases (Al Jazeera 2023b).

The Collectif Contre l'Islamophobie en Europe (CCIE), founded in Brussels in November 2020 to take over CCIF's work after the French government dissolved it, publishes detailed annual reports on Islamophobic incidents across Europe. Its 2024 report documents 1,037 incidents in France alone and offers some of the most rigorous available data on structural Islamophobia (CCIE 2024).

The Justice and Liberties For All Committee (CJL), led by Yasser Louati (formerly CCIF's spokesperson), continues civil rights advocacy from inside France.

The Ligue des Droits de l'Homme (LDH), France's oldest human rights organization, founded in 1898 during the Dreyfus Affair, has consistently joined Muslim civil society in litigation, including the successful 2016 challenge to the burkini bans at the Conseil d'État (Statewatch 2020).

Sport, Culture, and Direct Action

Les Hijabeuses is a Paris-based collective of female footballers who wear the hijab. Founded in 2020, it brought the formal complaint against the FFF hijab ban that was rejected by the Council of State in 2023; the case is now pending before the European Court of Human Rights (Amnesty International 2024). Co-founder Founé Diawara has framed the work as a human rights struggle: "Our fight is not political or religious but centred on our human right to participate in sports" (Amnesty International 2024).

Basket Pour Toutes plays the same role for basketball, a coalition formed in 2022 specifically in response to the basketball federation's hijab ban.

International Solidarity

The European Network Against Racism (ENAR) has coordinated international civil-society pressure on the French government, including a 2020 joint statement signed by 34 organizations across Europe condemning the dissolution of CCIF (ENAR 2020). Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch have published regular country reports documenting human rights violations affecting French Muslims (Amnesty International 2023, 2024; HRW 2016, 2025). The UN Human Rights Committee ruled in 2018 that France's full-face veil ban violated the human rights of those who wore the niqab (CNN 2023). The UN Special Rapporteur on freedom of religion or belief has raised repeated concerns (Amnesty International 2022).

These organizations exist to do what Said insisted is the only honest alternative to the dominant pattern: replace the "sensationalism, crude xenophobia, and insensitive belligerence" of mass-media discourse with "dialogue and exchange — both of which occur in scholarly debate, in artistic production, in the encounters between ordinary human beings who do business, interact, and generally talk to" (Said 1997, xlviii). That dialogue has not yet won in France, but it has been preserved as a possibility.

Political Voices

On the parliamentary left, La France Insoumise under Jean-Luc Mélenchon has been the largest political force consistently opposing the Islamophobic drift, attending the November 2019 March Against Islamophobia despite the political cost: accusations of "Islamo-leftism" (Jacobin 2025). The 2025 mass mobilization following the murder of Aboubakar Cissé in a southern French mosque drew approximately 2,000 people in La Grande-Combe and hundreds more in Paris's Place de la République (Jacobin 2025).

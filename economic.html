Section 06

Economic Restrictions

Discriminatory hiring, housing exclusion, surveillance of Muslim financial associations, and the consolidation of a Muslim "underclass."

Economic Islamophobia in France operates through multiple channels: discriminatory hiring, housing exclusion, surveillance of Muslim financial associations, and the legal and reputational targeting of Muslim-owned businesses.

Hiring Discrimination

The Adida-Laitin-Valfort (2010) audit study mentioned earlier found that a Muslim job applicant in France is 2.5 times less likely to receive a callback than an otherwise-identical Christian applicant. The follow-up 2020 study (Valfort 2020) refined the finding: religious Muslim men face the largest "religiosity penalty," consistent with employer perceptions of religious Muslim men as a "risk of religious radicalism." These patterns exemplify the kind of discrimination Martín-Muñoz identifies as the foundation of structural exclusion: "anti-Muslim tendencies described in the abovementioned report are reflected through discrimination in key areas for integration such as employment, education and housing" (Martín-Muñoz 2010, 23).

Housing Discrimination

The FRA 2024 survey found that 35 percent of French Muslims could not buy or rent a house because of discrimination, with rates rising to 46 percent for Muslims with disabilities (FRA 2024). Wagner and Petev (2024) documented that following Islamist terrorist attacks, Arab/Muslim hosts on online accommodation platforms in France lose between $5.5 million and $6.2 million in revenue, suggesting collective economic punishment of innocent individuals based on ethno-religious identification, precisely the pattern Martín-Muñoz identifies, in which "national and religious origin governs control and security criteria, reinforcing the social stigmatization of Muslim communities in Western countries" (Martín-Muñoz 2010, 26-27).

Banking and Financial Surveillance

The 2021 separatism law subjects religious associations (including mosques) to mandatory government review of their bank accounts and requires declaration of all donations from abroad. Muslim charitable organizations are required to sign "contracts of republican engagement" to receive any public funding, and four organizations have had subsidies withdrawn since 2021 for alleged violations (Sénat 2024). The 2020 dissolution of BarakaCity, a Muslim humanitarian aid organization, was justified in part on financial grounds.

Wage and Employment Gaps

Cesari has described the cumulative effect across Europe as the consolidation of a Muslim "underclass," a population whose lower socioeconomic position persists "even when their levels of education or resources are comparable to other immigrant groups" (Cesari 2013b). The FRA 2024 survey found that 41 percent of EU Muslims are overqualified for their jobs, compared to 22 percent of the general population (FRA 2024). As Martín-Muñoz warns, "discrimination, ghettos and contempt for the culture and identity of a people are the perfect breeding ground for hatred and radicalisation" (Martín-Muñoz 2010, 23): a feedback loop in which structural exclusion is then cited as evidence of cultural incompatibility.
